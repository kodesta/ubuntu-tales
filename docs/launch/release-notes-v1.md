# Ubuntu Tales — v1 release notes (draft)

*Not yet released. This documents the built scope for the launch announcement.*

## Read

- A reading home organised as publications (tribes): pick your roots, follow voices, and keep a
  reading list that travels with your account.
- Editorial reader with Lora typography, sentence-level read-along, adjustable text size and
  reading-progress memory.
- Archive with category filters, infinite scroll and griot (narrator) profiles.
- Shareable tale links with WhatsApp/X/Facebook previews (OG share page).

## Listen

- Narrated tales with a full player: seek, ±15 seconds, speed, ambience soundscape and lock-screen
  controls.
- Read-along highlighting driven by real sentence timings.
- Progressive AAC audio (64 kbps data-saver and 128 kbps), with the data-saver rendition chosen
  automatically on slow connections.

## Belong

- Accounts with verified email, rotating refresh sessions and clear privacy choices (roots are
  optional, analytics are opt-in).
- Saved tales, cross-device reading progress, listening statistics and streaks.
- Five emotional reactions and a completion screen that recommends the next tale.

## Contribute

- Anyone with a verified account can submit a tale, with rights confirmation and a licence choice.
- Editorial console for reviewers: stories, narration timing, moderation queue, story-of-the-day
  calendar, users, feature flags and an audit log.

## Read anywhere

- Installable PWA with an offline shell.
- Download a tale (text, cover and narration) for offline reading and listening, including seeking in
  downloaded audio.
- Queued progress writes replay when the connection returns.

## Privacy first

- No analytics before consent; no advertising; no data sales.
- Access tokens are memory-only; refresh tokens are httpOnly cookies.
- Roots/lineage are optional, consented and removable.

## Known limitations at v1

- Demo narration audio is synthesised for development; human recordings arrive with editorial
  readiness.
- Android/desktop browsers are the primary targets; iOS Safari is verified manually at launch.
- Native apps, comments, subscriptions and additional content languages are post-launch.
